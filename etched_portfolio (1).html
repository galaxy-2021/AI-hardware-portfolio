NVIDIA H100 / B200

General-Purpose AI Accelerator · $30K–$40K (B200) · ~87% Market Share

NVIDIA commands roughly 87% of the discrete AI GPU market.1 Its Blackwell B200 features a dual-die design with 208 billion transistors and 180 GB of HBM3e at 8 TB/s bandwidth — more than doubling the H100's 80 GB at 3.35 TB/s.2 NVIDIA reported ~$51.2 billion in data center revenue in Q3 FY2026 alone.3 The DGX B200 system delivers 15× the inference performance and 12× lower cost compared to DGX H100 for large MoE models.4

Architecture

Hopper (H100): 80 GB HBM3, 3.35 TB/s bandwidth, 4th-gen Tensor Cores with FP8. Transformer Engine. 700 W TDP.

Blackwell (B200): Dual GB100 dies connected by 10 TB/s inter-die link. 2nd-gen Transformer Engine with native FP4. 180 GB HBM3e, 8 TB/s bandwidth, 1000 W TDP. 9,000 TFLOPS FP4 dense.2

What's Working

  • CUDA ecosystem lock-in — 15+ years of software investment creates switching costs that protect market position. PyTorch, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM are all CUDA-optimized.
  • Memory capacity enables single-GPU serving. B200's 180 GB lets you run a full 70B model at FP16 on one GPU — H100 requires tensor parallelism across two.2
  • Proven at scale — deployed across every major AI lab for both training and inference.

What I'd Change

  • Price-performance for inference is suboptimal. At $30–40K per card,5 customers pay for FP64/FP32 training capability unused during inference. Autoregressive token generation is memory-bandwidth-bound, not compute-bound — the B200's massive FLOPS are architectural overkill for this workload.
  • Power trajectory is unsustainable. A100 (400W) → H100 (700W) → B200 (1000W) → B300 (1,400W). 24/7 inference makes every watt matter.
  • Software stack maturity lags new hardware. Real-world B200 benchmarks showed LLM inference gains smaller than expected because vLLM and similar frameworks weren't yet stable on Blackwell.6

Etched Comparison

Etched claims an 8-chip Sohu server generates 500,000+ tokens/sec on Llama 70B vs ~23,000 for 8×H100 — roughly 22× throughput.7 Etched estimates only 3.3% of H100 transistors handle the matrix multiplication that dominates transformer inference; Sohu dedicates nearly all silicon to this operation.8 The question: does the CUDA ecosystem's gravity outweigh raw performance advantages?

Google TPU v5e / v5p

Hyperscaler ASIC · Cloud-Only · Closed Ecosystem

Google's Tensor Processing Units are the original bet on domain-specific AI acceleration. TPU v5e targets cost-efficient inference; v5p handles training in pods of up to 4,096 chips. Google co-designs models (Gemini, PaLM) with hardware, enabling optimizations third-party GPU vendors cannot match. Amazon's Trainium/Inferentia handled ~35% of new AWS AI workloads in 2025, demonstrating hyperscaler appetite for custom silicon.3

Architecture

Systolic Array Design: Data flows through a grid of multiply-accumulate units, maximizing utilization for matrix operations. Each chip is purpose-built around this pattern.

v5e (inference): Lower power, higher density per pod. Designed for cost-efficient inference at hyperscale.

v5p (training): Higher performance, 4,096-chip pods with custom high-speed interconnect (ICI).

What's Working

  • Vertical integration — Google co-designs models and hardware, enabling layer-level optimizations impossible for third-party vendors.
  • Cost efficiency at hyperscale — custom silicon economics are compelling at Google's volume. TPU v5e delivers lower cost-per-inference than H100 for supported workloads.
  • Proof of ASIC viability — validates the strategic direction for companies like Etched building specialized chips for the broader market.

What I'd Change

  • Closed ecosystem limits adoption. TPUs are only available via Google Cloud. No on-premise option. Multi-cloud enterprises are locked out.
  • Software ecosystem lags NVIDIA. JAX/TensorFlow have strong TPU support, but PyTorch — the dominant framework — still has weaker TPU integration.
  • Limited transparency. Google publishes fewer architectural details than NVIDIA, making optimization harder for external teams.

Etched Comparison

TPU proves the technical case for AI ASICs but highlights the distribution challenge. Google's closed model leaves the broader market underserved — cloud providers, AI startups, and enterprises wanting on-premise or multi-cloud deployment have no TPU option. Etched can be "TPU for everyone else" — ASIC performance as a purchasable product without cloud lock-in.

Groq LPU (Language Processing Unit)

Deterministic Latency · Inference-Specialized · Acquired by NVIDIA ($20B, Dec 2025)

Groq pioneered deterministic inference — a chip where every operation's timing is known at compile time. The LPU delivered 300+ tokens/sec on Llama 2 70B, roughly 10× faster than H100 clusters.9 NVIDIA acquired Groq for $20 billion on December 24, 2025 — its largest acquisition ever.10 At GTC 2026, NVIDIA unveiled the Groq 3 LPU targeting 1,500 tokens/sec for agentic AI, with 40 PB/s bandwidth across 256-LPU racks.10

Architecture

Temporal Computing: Operations scheduled at compile time, not runtime. Eliminates dynamic dispatch overhead. Deterministic execution — every inference takes exactly the same time.

Memory: Hundreds of MB on-chip SRAM as primary weight storage (not cache). Cuts latency by feeding compute at full speed. Groq 3 LPU: 128 GB SRAM per 256-LPU rack.10

Scale: Running Llama 2 70B requires ~576 LPUs in coordination, creating substantial capital requirements.9

What's Working

  • Deterministic latency. Sub-300ms time-to-first-token with zero jitter enables real-time voice, translation, and agents impossible with variable-latency GPUs.
  • Enterprise traction. 1.9M+ developers on GroqCloud. Enterprise deployments at Dropbox, Volkswagen, Riot Games. Meta partnered for official Llama API in April 2025.9
  • $20B acquisition validates inference specialization. NVIDIA paid 7× Groq's $2.8B valuation — the clearest signal that inference optimization is strategically essential.10

What I'd Change

  • SRAM limits model size per chip. Hundreds of MB SRAM vs 180 GB HBM3e on B200 means larger models need many more chips, increasing system cost.
  • Narrow initial use case. Deterministic latency commands premium value for real-time applications, but broader batch-inference workloads may prioritize cost-per-token over latency.
  • Post-acquisition uncertainty. Integration into NVIDIA's stack may position Groq as a coprocessor rather than standalone, limiting independent adoption.

Etched Comparison

The Groq acquisition reshapes the landscape. With Groq inside NVIDIA, Etched becomes the most prominent independent inference ASIC company. Their approaches differ: Groq optimized for latency determinism across architectures; Etched optimizes for throughput on transformers specifically. Etched's claimed 500,000 tok/s on Llama 70B dwarfs Groq's 300 tok/s, though Etched's numbers remain to be independently verified at scale.7, 9

Analysis Framework: Why TCO Over Benchmark Wars

These teardowns prioritize Total Cost of Ownership and strategic positioning over raw TFLOPS comparisons because AI infrastructure buying decisions are made on economics, not spec sheets. A chip that's 2× faster but costs 5× more and consumes 3× power loses on TCO. Understanding the full cost stack — CapEx, OpEx, switching costs, ecosystem lock-in — reveals where new entrants can compete.

AI Inference Semiconductor Market

Market sizing, competitive positioning, and strategic analysis of the AI inference chip landscape. Identifying where specialized ASICs compete against incumbent GPUs.

Market Sizing Framework

TAM (Total Addressable Market): The global AI chip market was valued at ~$94 billion in 2025 and is projected to exceed $1 trillion by 2035 at a 28% CAGR.T1 Inference workloads now account for roughly two-thirds of all AI compute, up from one-third in 2023.T2

SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market): The AI inference chip market specifically was valued at $31 billion in 2024, projected to reach $167 billion by 2032 at 28.25% CAGR.T3 Deloitte estimates inference-optimized chips will exceed $50 billion in 2026.T2

SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): Excluding hyperscaler-captive silicon (Google TPU, Meta MTIA, Amazon Trainium/Inferentia), the accessible market for external inference vendors — cloud providers, AI startups, enterprises — is estimated at $20–30 billion annually by 2027.

Porter's Five Forces

Market Structure Analysis

Threat of New Entrants: HIGH BARRIERS

$100M+ to tape out on advanced nodes, 2–3 year design cycles, and ecosystem requirements create formidable barriers. TSMC now allocates 28%+ of wafer production to AI chips.T4 Venture funding (Etched $500M, Cerebras ~$20B IPO target) has partially lowered capital barriers.

Supplier Power: MODERATE-HIGH

TSMC dominates advanced-node manufacturing. Etched's Sohu uses TSMC 4nm.T5 Alternative fabs (Samsung, Intel Foundry) lag on leading-edge. Mutual dependency — AI chips are TSMC's fastest-growing segment — moderates power somewhat.

Buyer Power: MODERATE

Large buyers (hyperscalers) can threaten vertical integration — Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft all build custom chips. But CUDA switching costs and engineering retraining limit leverage. NVIDIA's pricing umbrella ($30–40K per B200) creates room for alternatives.

Threat of Substitutes: MODERATE

Software optimization (quantization, pruning, distillation) can reduce compute needs. But AI scaling laws — including test-time compute — continue driving demand. Deloitte projects compute demand will grow as inference workloads expand.T2

Competitive Rivalry: INTENSE

NVIDIA's $20B Groq acquisition signals willingness to eliminate threats through M&A. Multiple well-funded startups and tech giants compete across positioning strategies. AMD earned ~$5.6B from AI chips in 2025.T4

Etched's Strategic Opening

Why Now? Three forces converge: (1) Transformer architecture consolidation reduces ASIC risk. (2) Inference economics — 24/7 production workloads at two-thirds of AI compute — favor cost-efficient specialized chips. (3) NVIDIA's pricing umbrella creates room for entrants with better price-performance on inference.

Wedge Strategy: Target AI companies scaling transformer inference who feel GPU cost pain but lack resources for in-house ASICs. Etched raised $500M at $5B valuation in January 2026, led by Stripes with Peter Thiel and Ribbit Capital.T5

Key Risk: Architecture shift away from transformers. NVIDIA's Groq acquisition also enables an integrated GPU training + LPU inference stack. Etched CEO Gavin Uberti: "If transformers go away, our company dies."T6

Technical Research

Transformer Architecture Convergence Analysis

Quantitative analysis of how transformer architectures have evolved and consolidated from 2017–2026. The key question for ASIC viability: is the market converging on a stable architecture?

Research Methodology

Systematic review of 53 major language models released between 2017 and 2025, tracking architectural features: encoder/decoder structure, attention mechanisms, normalization layers, position encodings, and activation functions. Primary source: Jun Yu Tan's open dataset of publication dates and architectural specifications,A1 supplemented by model cards from Hugging Face, original papers on arXiv, and technical blog posts from leading AI labs.

Key Findings

Architectural Consolidation Trends

95%

Decoder-Only Architecture

~95% of major models released 2024–2026 use decoder-only transformers, up from ~60% in 2020–2022. Encoder-decoder (T5-style) and encoder-only (BERT-style) architectures have been largely abandoned for generative workloads — the workloads that drive inference demand.

High Convergence
MHA

Multi-Head Attention + KV-Sharing

The fundamental attention mechanism from 2017 remains universal. The evolution is in KV-cache optimization: Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) and Multi-Query Attention (MQA) are now standard in production models (Llama 3, Mistral, Gemma) — reducing memory overhead during inference without quality loss.A1

Core StabilityGQA/MQA
RMS

RMSNorm Standardization

RMSNorm has displaced LayerNorm in nearly all newer model families. It drops mean-centering, making it 10–50% more computationally efficient with similar stability.A2 Adopted by Llama, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen, and most open-weight models since 2023. Pre-norm convention (normalizing before attention/FFN) is now universal.

Incremental Evolution
RoPE

Rotary Position Embedding

RoPE is now the dominant position encoding, replacing absolute sinusoidal and learned encodings. It encodes position via rotation matrices in the attention computation, enabling better generalization to longer sequences than seen during training.A1 ALiBi survives as a niche alternative.

Standard Practice
GLU

SwiGLU Activation Functions

Gated Linear Units with Swish activation have replaced ReLU/GELU in feed-forward layers. The gating mechanism improves expressiveness while keeping FLOP count roughly constant via reduced intermediate dimension (~8/3× expansion vs original 4×).A1 Adopted by Llama, PaLM, Gemini, Mistral since late 2022.

Convergent Default
MoE

Mixture of Experts: Remaining Frontier

MoE is the one area where architecture design remains actively experimental. Routing strategies, expert counts (8 to 256+), and granularity vary significantly (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V2, GPT-4). This is where the converged dense recipe gives way to genuine diversity — but even MoE models use transformer attention as their core primitive.A1

Active Experimentation

The 2023–2026 Consensus Stack

As the analysis of 53 models documents, influential open-weight model families have converged on a de facto standard recipe: decoder-only + pre-norm RMSNorm + RoPE + SwiGLU FFN + KV-sharing attention (GQA/MQA) + bias-free layers.A1 This convergence partly reflects hardware-software co-optimization — FlashAttention, fused RMSNorm kernels, and fused SwiGLU kernels are well-optimized for this specific recipe, creating path dependence.A1 For Etched, this is strategically important: if the recipe is stable because of hardware co-optimization, then hardware designed around this recipe has a self-reinforcing advantage.

Alternative Architecture Risk Assessment

State Space Models (SSMs): Mamba and S4 offer sub-quadratic scaling for long sequences, but only a handful of production models use pure SSM architectures vs. 50+ active transformer families. SSMs haven't demonstrated clear superiority on standard benchmarks.A3

Hybrid Architectures: Jamba (AI21) combines Mamba blocks with attention. xLSTM-7B achieves 3.5× faster training than comparable transformers.A3 These hybrids still rely on attention for quality — they augment the transformer rather than eliminate it.

Diffusion LLMs: Google's Gemini Diffusion explores non-autoregressive text generation. Speed advantages exist but quality gaps remain.A4

Overall: Alternatives face steep adoption barriers. Transformers benefit from 8+ years of optimization across hardware, software, and training recipes. Displacement requires practical superiority across diverse production tasks — a bar no alternative has cleared.

Implications for Etched's ASIC Strategy

Convergence Score: 8.5/10 (Very High)

The transformer is consolidating around a narrow set of patterns. Architectural diversity has decreased markedly since 2020. MoE is the primary area of experimentation, but MoE models still use transformer attention as their core compute primitive.

Risk Timeline: Short term (1–2 years): very low risk — transformer dominance accelerating. Medium term (3–5 years): low-moderate — alternatives exist but haven't proven superior at scale. Long term (5+ years): moderate — new architectures possible, but transformers are evolving too.

Mitigation: Etched's 3–5 year product horizon aligns with high-confidence transformer dominance. Revenue from initial Sohu product ($500M raised, $5B valuation) can fund next-gen architectures if needed. First-mover learning on ASIC design and TSMC manufacturing creates durable advantage even if architecture eventually shifts.
